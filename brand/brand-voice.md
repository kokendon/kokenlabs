# Voice Guide: Koken Labs
Built 2026-11-05 from: kokenlabs.ai (Home, Services, About, including the About page's "How we think") · Status: documented as-is from the site, not owner-confirmed

# Page 1: the part people use
## Who this sounds like
A senior engineer who has shipped this before, talking to another builder: short sentences, real numbers, no magic-AI pitch.

## Voice attributes
| Attribute | Means | Doesn't mean | Real example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct over clever | Short sentences, the claim first, plain words | Curt or cold with a worried buyer | "Tell us what you want working in production. We'll tell you how fast we can ship it." (Home) |
| Show the work | Specifics, numbers, timelines, named tools | Dumping jargon or tech stacks for show | "Clear price & timeline in ~2 business days." (Home) |
| Confident, never hype | State what we do and back it up | Bragging, superlatives, "revolutionary" | "We don't promise revolution. We promise it works, and then we prove it in production." (About) |
| Speak to makers | Peer to peer, assumes the reader builds things | Talking down, or insider slang that shuts buyers out | "We work best with people who build things and want a partner who moves at their pace." (About) |

## We are / We are not
- Direct, not blunt
- Specific, not technical for its own sake
- Confident, not loud
- Edgy in look, not careless in substance
- A partner, not a vendor pitching a "journey"

## Words we use / words we avoid
- Use: ship, shipped, production, build, working software, weeks, fixed-scope, productized, clear price, real engineering, frontier models, agents, automation
- Avoid: journey, transformation, synergy, unlock, elevate, seamless, game-changer, revolutionary, leverage (as a verb), cutting-edge, "AI magic"

## Never (incl. AI tells)
- Openers like "In today's fast-paced world…" or "Ever wondered…?"
- delve, elevate, unlock, seamless, game-changer, navigate the landscape
- Reflexive groups of three; stacked em dashes; closing summaries that repeat the piece
- "It's not X, it's Y" as a habit (the site uses "weeks, not quarters" once as a signature line; don't multiply it)
- All-caps hype, more than one exclamation mark per piece
- "Koken Labs is thrilled/pleased to announce…"
- Claims we can't back: guaranteed ROI figures, client results we don't have permission to share

## Rules we must follow
- Name: "Koken Labs", two words, both capitalized. Lowercase "koken" with the ō appears only inside the logo.
- Tagline: "From chaos to clarity." with the period. Secondary line: "Intelligence, shipped."
- Only claim speeds and prices we actually quote. No client names or results without permission.

## Paste-in voice prompt
Write as Koken Labs, an applied AI studio that ships AI products, agents and automation into production in weeks. Sound like a senior engineer talking to another builder. Be direct over clever: short sentences, the point first, plain words. Show the work: use specific numbers, timelines and named tools instead of adjectives. Be confident, never hype: no superlatives, no "revolutionary", no "journey" or "transformation". Speak to makers as a peer. Never use: delve, elevate, unlock, seamless, game-changer, "in today's fast-paced world", groups of three, stacked em dashes, all-caps hype or more than one exclamation mark. Write the name as "Koken Labs". Close with a clear, low-pressure call to action such as "Start a build".

# Appendix
## Tone dials (1–5)

|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 2 | 3 | 4 |

## Rhythm, formatting, openings and closings
- Sentences mostly 6–15 words; fragments are fine for punch ("Weeks to production, not quarters.").
- Paragraphs 1–3 sentences. Digits for numbers. Mono-style section labels ("01 — The gap").
- Open with the reader's problem or the claim. Close with one direct CTA: "Start a build →", "Tell us the problem and we'll scope it."
- The site uses emoji as service-card icons. Keep them out of headlines and body copy.

## Signature moves
1. Contrast with the big firms: "Big-firm capability. None of the drag." (Home)
2. Put the time and price up front: "A two-week, fixed-price sprint…" (Services)
3. Name the anti-pattern: "Most AI work is still a slide deck." (Home)
4. Promise, then proof: "We promise it works, and then we prove it in production." (About)

## Tone by situation
| Situation | Shift | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| First reply to a new lead | Warm, fast, specific next step | "Thanks for the detail. We can scope this on a 20-minute call and send a fixed price within 2 business days." |
| 5-star review reply | Short thanks, name the result | "Glad the intake agent is pulling its weight. Thanks for building it with us." |
| 1-star review reply | No humor, own it, move offline | "That's not the experience we aim for. I'd like to fix it. Can you email hello@kokenlabs.ai so we can go through it?" |
| Delay notice | Plain facts, new date, why | "The integration testing found an edge case. New ship date is Friday. Here's what changed." |
| Technical explainer | More detail, same short sentences | Lead with what it does, then how, then the limits. |

## Before → after
- "We leverage cutting-edge AI to transform your business." → "We build AI agents that answer leads and file tickets, and we ship them in weeks."
- "Contact us to begin your AI journey." → "Tell us the problem. We'll send a price and a timeline."
- "Our innovative solutions deliver seamless results." → "Fixed scope, fixed price, working software in production."
